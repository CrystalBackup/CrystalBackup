M3.1 Operator Throughput Audit

Why the full crucible suite kept failing differently on every run — measured on live infrastructure before a single line was changed, then re-measured against the fixes.

Method released 0.3.0 operator, unmodified, on a live RKE2 / Ceph crucible Instrument controller-runtime metrics + two restic scaling benchmarks Date 2026-07-25 Outcome 0.3.1
Not O(snapshots), not resources — a controller waking itself in a loop

The roadmap’s working hypothesis was that discovery’s restic snapshots re-scan grew with the repository. The measurement said otherwise: discovery’s single reconcile worker was ~100 % saturated for an entire run at three snapshots — reconcile_time_seconds{controller="discovery"} summed to 1991 s inside a 1980 s window, while every other controller combined stayed under 100 s. The operator was never resource-bound (CPU ~0.01 core, RSS ~290 MB). The cost was wall-time on serialized blocking reconciles, and the multiplier was a self-trigger loop: discovery wrote its own inventory status onto the object it watches, and the watch had no predicate.

Self-trigger loop
~10× multiplier		Discovery writes snapshotCount, namespacesPresent and lastDiscoveryTime onto the very BackupRepository it watches, unfiltered — so each write returned as an event and re-enqueued it at once. The flakiness chain

The most useful thing the validation produced was not a number — it was the answer to why the suite fails differently every run:

1A restore leaves a PVC carrying an orphaned snapshot.storage.kubernetes.io/pvc-as-source-protection finalizer — with zero VolumeSnapshots left in the cluster, so it protects nothing.
2That PVC never finishes deleting, so its namespace stays Terminating indefinitely (observed: 22 minutes and counting).
3Discovery’s projection pass hits it, fails, and aborts before recording the inventory — freezing discovery completely.
4Whichever spec next needed a fresh inventory is the one that fails. A different victim every run.

Step 3 is fixed here. Steps 1–2 are a leak-check invariant violation and are deferred, deliberately un-guessed.

Deferred to 0.3.2 (tracked, not guessed)

The leak that creates the stuck namespace — an orphaned finalizer, and a restore twin PV that failed to mount with internal RBD image not found after two earlier restores on the same volume had succeeded. The twin copies the volume handle verbatim under a Retain policy precisely so a twin can never destroy the user’s volume, so either the image vanished for a Ceph-side reason or something in teardown released a PV under a Delete policy — two explanations calling for opposite fixes, which is exactly why it ships as a reproduction plan rather than a patch. Also deferred: any projection error still discards the consumed inventory, forcing a full re-listing on retry; and a warm/incremental inventory (cold O(N) → warm O(Δ)) if a repository ever grows into the thousands of snapshots.

Method: the released 0.3.0 operator was deployed unmodified and scraped over a whole full-suite run, so the instrumentation could not perturb what it measured; restic snapshots was then benchmarked separately against a local filesystem repository and the real Hetzner S3 to separate compute from network. Fixes gated by make test (unit + envtest, including -race), golangci-lint, and a re-run of the same crucible measurement. Full write-up: docs/audit-m3.1-throughput.md.
